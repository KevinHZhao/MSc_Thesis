In studies on gene association, two major forms of observational study design have emerged: the case-control study, and the case-parents study.  While the case-control study is the more common of the two, it suffers under population stratification.  The case-parents design, where each "case" child is given two "controls" in their parents, remedies this.  From case-parent data, we may assess not only the risk a child's genes may bring upon themselves, but also the risk a mother's genes may bring upon their child (such as during fetal development).  However, one may wish to include other factors such as mating asymmetry, gene-environment interaction effects, and imprinting effects, where a child's risk from having a genetic factor is dependent on that factor's parent of origin.  To account for imprinting, we must solve a missing data problem using the EM algorithm.  While this entire process has been outlined before, and software to incorporates some of these factors exist, no software is presently available for researchers to efficiently model all of these considerations simultaneously.  For our research project, we will create a freely available R package allowing anyone to model these genetic effects and obtain statistically sound conclusions.  !!
